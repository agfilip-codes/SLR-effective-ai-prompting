# Effective Prompting in AI Systems: A Systematic Literature Review

Systematic literature review synthesizing 14 papers across NLP and HCI research on what actually makes AI prompting effective. Completed as a class assignment.

## Key finding

NLP and HCI research measure different things while both calling it "prompting effectiveness." NLP research typically assumes users already know exactly what they want to ask for. HCI research shows real users almost never do, and effectiveness in practice depends heavily on iteration, not just initial prompt design.

## Takeaways

- Prompt strategy matters less than commonly assumed; task type and model choice are bigger factors in output quality
- No prompting technique fully compensates for a model's underlying weaknesses
- Users who get the most value from AI systems aren't necessarily the best prompt writers. They iterate on prompts, switch between models, question outputs, and evaluate results critically
- This suggests a conceptual gap between how prompting effectiveness is benchmarked in controlled studies versus how it plays out in real-world use

## Method

Sourced primarily from arXiv, with a small number from other outlets. Searched using queries including:
- "Prompt engineering methods" for NLP survey literature
- "Prompt engineering human response benchmark" for NLP- and HCI-oriented examples
- "Human-AI interaction prompt behavior" for HCI research
- "How users verify AI outputs" for additional HCI research
